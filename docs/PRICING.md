# Pricing and the first client

Written for the UK/AU focus. Currency GBP, with AUD reference points for Australian
clients. Prices live in `app/pricing/page.tsx` and `prisma/seed.ts` — change them in both.

---

## The market you are pricing against

Three different price anchors exist, and buyers compare you to the wrong one unless you
name it first.

**Cheap AI subscriptions** — Goodcall roughly $79 / $129 / $249 per month (caps at
100 / 250 / 500 unique customers, $0.50 overage); Sameday AI $449–$789 per month; UK
TradeReceptionist from around £49/month; AU Frontly from around A$149/month. These are
chat widgets with a knowledge base you fill in yourself. Their cost of goods for a
busy number is real: roughly $60–90 a month at 500 minutes.

**Freelancers** — Upwork median fixed budget for “ai automation” work is around $150,
median hourly around $29.50. This is the anchor you must avoid, because it prices a
tool, not an outcome.

**Agencies** — a UK digital agency of any size tends to quote £8,000–£25,000 for a site
plus retainer. This prices out an owner-run business.

The gap between £150 and £15,000 is where a solo studio can charge £1,450–£6,000 for a
fixed scope and still be the obvious middle option.

> Verify these figures before quoting from them. They were compiled in September 2026
> and competitor pricing moves. Check each vendor's pricing page the week you send
> proposals, and never quote a competitor's price you have not seen yourself.

## The price ladder

| Tier | GBP (one-off) | AUD reference | Monthly (optional care) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Core** — site, 5–7 pages | £1,450 | A$2,750 | £95 | A business with a working offer and a weak site |
| **System** — site + AI agent + follow-up | £3,400 | A$6,400 | £180 | Losing enquiries outside office hours |
| **Managed** — up to three automations + support | £5,900 | A$11,000 | from £450 | Wants someone accountable for the whole system |

Add-ons: extra page £140 · copywriting £90/page · second language £450 · booking
integration £320 · rush +35%.

**Why these numbers:**

- £1,450 sits above the “cheap freelancer” signal and well below agency quotes, which is
  exactly the gap a solo studio can defend. A site at £800 reads as a template; at
  £1,450 it reads as work.
- £3,400 for System is deliberately more than a year of a £79/month subscription. You are
  not selling the bot — you are selling the wiring, the boundaries, the testing and the
  six weeks of accountability. Say that plainly; it is the honest justification.
- Care plans are £95–£450, priced against what a small business already pays for hosting
  and a bookkeeper. They should feel like insurance, not a subscription.
- The 50/50 payment split (deposit to book, balance on launch) is doing two jobs: it funds
  your time and it filters tyre-kickers.

## What to say when they compare you to £49/month

> “You can absolutely buy that bot for £49 a month — it will answer questions about your
> opening hours. What it will not know is that a 12-panel install in Leeds goes to a
> specific person, or that your pricing has a minimum call-out. Getting that right is
> the work, and it is why we charge a setup fee and put the account in your name. If we
> stop being useful, you stop paying us and the system keeps running.”

That is the whole pitch. Do not discount; change the scope instead.

## Currency and tax

- Quote UK clients in GBP, ex-VAT. Register for VAT only when you cross the threshold —
  it is £90,000 of turnover at the time of writing, so not yet.
- Quote Australian clients in AUD at an agreed fixed rate, and state clearly whether the
  price includes GST. Australian business clients generally want a tax invoice with your
  ABN. If you are not registered for GST, put “No GST” on the invoice.
- Do not add a currency-conversion surcharge. Absorb it in the rate you set.
- Pakistan-side: keep the business bank account separate from personal and record
  every incoming payment. This matters more later than it feels now.

## Cold outreach is slow. Plan for it.

The uncomfortable arithmetic: cold email converts to *genuinely interested* at roughly
0.24% on large samples — about 400 sends per interested reply. LinkedIn connection
acceptance runs around 28.5%, which is better, but still slow.

That means a first client in the *next 30 days* is realistic only through warm channels
or unusually good targeting. A cold-email-only plan usually lands client one in 45–90
days. Plan for both:

| Channel | Weekly | Realistic 30-day yield |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Warm network / past contacts | 20 personal messages | 1–3 calls |
| LinkedIn (targeted, 20/day) | 100 invitations | 5–8 accepted, 1–2 calls |
| Cold email (UK/AU, 30/day) | 150 sends | ~0.3 interested reply |
| Local business groups / directories | 2 posts | 0–1 lead |
| Upwork / freelance platforms | 5 proposals | 0–1 job (do not make it primary) |

**Legality, briefly:** B2B cold email is legal in the US and Australia (with opt-out),
and legal in the UK only to corporates — sole traders need prior consent, and PECR covers
social DMs. Germany is unlawful without consent (§7(2) Nr.2 UWG, with Abmahnung costs and
fines up to €300,000). Never cold WhatsApp anywhere; it breaches Meta's terms.

## Thirty days, realistically

**Days 1–3 — make the site real.** Replace the placeholder case studies or unpublish
them. Publish one genuine testimonial. Connect the review and social APIs. Add real
Open Graph images. Submit the sitemap. *Do not send outreach to a site showing invented
case studies.*

**Days 3–7 — build the list.** 150 UK/AU businesses matching this profile: a working
offer, a site older than three years, no online booking, and a visible contact form with
no reply automation. That last filter matters — if they already answer instantly, there
is no problem to sell.

**Days 5–21 — outreach, in parallel.** 20 warm messages, 20 LinkedIn invites a day, 30
cold emails a day, one useful post a week. Every reply within two hours. The first
client usually comes from the warm list, not the cold one.

**Days 10–21 — run two free 20-minute audits.** Offer the diagnostic, not the build. A
short written summary of where their enquiries leak is the best sales asset you have,
and it costs you 40 minutes.

**Days 21–30 — convert.** Send a fixed-scope proposal within 24 hours of a call. One
price, one start date, one paragraph of scope. If they hesitate, offer Core (£1,450)
rather than discounting System.

## Where to be honest with yourself

- **Do not promise a first client in this month as a certainty.** You can commit to the
  inputs (600 sends, 40 calls attempted, 2 audits) — the output depends on the market.
- **The first client will probably be underpriced.** That is fine once, if you trade the
  discount for a testimonial, a case study with numbers, and permission to use their
  name. Write that exchange into the deal.
- **Do not take a second underpriced client.** The first one is a reference; the second
  one is a business model.
- **Do not sell the bot alone.** The £79/month market owns that. Sell the integration,
  the boundaries and the accountability — that is the part nobody can get from a
  subscription.

---

## What changes the pricing later

Once you have three case studies with numbers and a handful of reviews, the same work
is worth 30–50% more. Raise Core to £1,900 and System to £4,200 before you raise
Managed — the entry tier is where the price comparison happens, and it is where a
higher price most improves who enquires.
